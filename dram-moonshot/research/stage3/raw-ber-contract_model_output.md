## 3. Erasure flags: the key unknown, modelled

Physics of the flag. A second, earlier sample (reduced tRCD, or a delayed-latch SA) mis-resolves bits whose |dV| is inside a window delta. Two consequences the concept text misses:

(a) False flags. Errors live in a density tail that rises toward the mean, so more correct bits sit just above threshold than wrong bits just below. Margin errors (Gaussian, signal at z sigma):

| delta (sigma) | delta mV | flagged share of errors | false flags per error | flags per 592-b codeword at 1e-4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 1.8 | 64% | 1.6 | 0.13 |
| 0.50 | 3.6 | 88% | 5.4 | 0.37 |
| 1.00 | 7.1 | 99% | 31.7 | 1.94 |
| 1.50 | 10.7 | 100% | 131.4 | 7.84 |

False flags outnumber caught errors 1.6-130x. An erased bit costs 1 unit of distance against 2 for an unflagged error, so in the deep tail (target 2e-19) erasures still win while the flag window is narrow (delta ~0.25-0.5 sigma, <0.4 flags per codeword); at delta >=1.5 sigma the false flags eat the gain. The best use is soft information: hard-decode first, then erase or Chase-flip the few flagged positions (<=8 here: <=256 trial decodes on a slow path that <1e-10 of reads take).

(b) Refresh latching. A retention error that occurs before the next refresh is sensed as the wrong value and restored to a full, confident rail. For long-resident data, retention and VRT errors are therefore almost all confident (unflaggable). Only bits read within their first refresh period after a write can be caught near threshold, with flagged share 1-(1+r)^-k (r = window / full bitline swing ~0.05-0.2, k = tail slope ~3-5): 14-60%. Flags are a margin-error tool (new cells), not a refresh tool.

Decoder comparison at raw BER 1e-4, delta = 0.5 sigma, 512 data bits, BCH m=10, target 2.0e-19: minimum t (check bits = 10 t) versus the confident (unflaggable) share of errors. THIS IS THE SENSITIVITY TABLE ON THE KEY UNKNOWN.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 9 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 70 | 1.73e-04 | 25.6 | 2.57 |
| 0.10 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 70 | 1.61e-04 | 25.9 | 2.61 |
| 0.25 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 8 | 80 | 1.52e-04 | 26.3 | 2.66 |
| 0.50 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 80 | 1.06e-04 | 27.7 | 2.81 |
| 0.75 | 9 | 10 | 9 | 9 | 90 | 7.59e-05 | 29.4 | 3.00 |
| 1.00 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 90 | 5.89e-05 | 50.2 | 5.45 |

Reading: with every error flaggable, Chase cuts check bits 90 -> 70 (1.29x) and lets a t=8 code run at 1.7e-04 raw BER instead of 5.9e-05 (hard): dV 27.5 -> 25.6 mV, i.e. flags add ~8% on top of the code's 1.88x. With half the errors confident the saving shrinks to 90 -> 80 bits; with all confident, flags are worth nothing and no margin is banked (the confident errors consume the budget). The concept's 'd=5 code, 3.9%' for 4 erasures ignores false flags and the realistic target.

## 4. Correlated bursts: how much clustering a line code survives

| 0.500 | 8 | <=1.00e-08 | 9.68e-05 | 1.47e-06 |

Reading: under a 2e-19 target a binary line code is fragile to clustering: 1% of error bits in 3-bit clusters already cuts BCH-12 headroom several-fold, and any 8-bit clusters kill it. A byte-symbol RS code in the same side band is ~6x weaker on iid bits but indifferent to byte-confined bursts and keeps chipkill. The contract must therefore specify error geometry (cluster-size spectrum per codeword), not only raw BER, and the controller should pick BCH or RS per device grade. Cluster spectrum is a first-order measurement. With a chip already marked dead, RS headroom falls to ~1e-6, so the controller must drop that rank back to <=256 ms refresh until the DIMM is replaced (graceful degradation rule for the contract).

## 8. Value: energy, bandwidth, density, cost

At 1000 ms: saves 0.46 W per 64 GB RDIMM -> 26 kWh over 5 yr at PUE 1.3 = $2.60 ($0.041/GB, 2.7% of $1.48/GB).
Fleet scale: 7.1 MW saved per exabyte of server DRAM (before PUE).
  16Gb: refresh-blocked time 7.6% -> 0.48% (bandwidth/latency recovered ~7.1 pts)
  32Gb: refresh-blocked time 10.5% -> 0.67% (bandwidth/latency recovered ~9.8 pts)
  64Gb: refresh-blocked time 41.0% -> 2.63% (bandwidth/latency recovered ~38.4 pts)

New-die check-bit cost: t=9 line BCH = 17.6% vs on-die 6.25% -> same 1b cell gives 0.404 Gb/mm2 (0.90x of 0.447). The contract pays for itself only through the cells it enables (shorter pillar, 3D tiers), never on today's planar cell.
Commodity path: the 25% ECC side band already exists on RDIMMs, so the contract adds zero bits there; the choice is BCH-12 (6x more random-bit headroom, no chipkill) or RS-8 (keeps chipkill, section 4).

## 9. GO / NO-GO for the decisive experiment

Measured on 50-100 DDR4 modules (no on-die ECC: raw physics) + retention-reject DIMMs, DRAM Bender, 45/65/85 C, 0.25-2 s refresh, truncated-tRAS half-charge restore, reduced-tRCD double reads as the flag proxy, >=4 weeks of VRT soak, hammer with controller RFM:

GO-A (refresh, commodity): measured BER at 0.5 s / 85 C (equivalently ~2 s / 65 C), including VRT growth over a 1 h scrub, <= 3.3e-04 with <= 0.41% of error bits in >=3-bit clusters per codeword (BCH-12), or <= 5.4e-05 with clusters confined to bytes/chips (RS-8, chipkill kept). Either gives a >=7.7x refresh-energy cut and refresh-blocked time 7.6% -> <1% on 16Gb dies.
GO-B (margin, new cells): at emulated half-charge raw BER 1e-4, >=50% of errors flagged at a <=0.5-sigma window (<=6 false flags per error), so Chase needs t<=8 (80 bits) where hard decoding needs t=9 (90 bits); per-codeword error counts within 2x of Poisson out to 6 errors per 512 b.
NO-GO: largest safe interval <256 ms at 65 C even with RS-8 (RAIDR-class profiling already gets ~4x without ECC), or >10% of error events spanning more than one byte (not modelled here: such clusters need chip-level symbols and would pull the interval back toward RAIDR-class gains), or <25% of half-charge errors flaggable (flags then save <=10 check bits and the SA flag latch is not worth 3-7% die area). Revival condition for a NO-GO-A: oxide/IGZO cells, whose retention tail sits 1e3-1e4x to the right, re-run the same test.

GO/NO-GO LINE: GO if the measured error stream fits the 128-bit side band (BCH-12 <= 3.3e-04 iid, or RS-8 <= 5.4e-05 clustered) at the 2e-19/read target with refresh >= 0.5 s at 85 C, AND >=50% of half-charge errors are flaggable; NO-GO if the largest safe interval is <256 ms at 65 C.

## Headline

- Target 2.0e-19 per 64 B read; the concept's t=8 is ~100x short; t=9 (17.6%) needed at 1e-4 with hard decisions.
- A commodity DDR5 ECC RDIMM's existing 128-bit side band takes iid raw BER up to 3.3e-04 (BCH-12) or 5.4e-05 with byte-confined clusters and chipkill (RS-8); behind DDR5 on-die SEC, internal BER up to 1.7e-04. BCH-12 tolerates only ~0.41% of error bits in 3-bit clusters at 1e-4.
- Refresh interval at 85 C, iid / clustered: pow3 3.59 / 1.97 s; lognormal 1.53 / 0.84 s; pow5 0.72 / 0.50 s. At 1 s: refresh 0.49 -> 0.03 W per 64 GB (15x), $0.041/GB of energy over 5 yr; tRFC loss 7.6% -> 0.5% (16Gb).
- Margin: 1e-4 buys 7 -> 3.72 sigma (50 -> 26.6 mV, Cs 5.4 -> 2.7 fF). Flags add only ~8% more (Chase t=7 vs hard t=9) and nothing for refresh-latched errors.
