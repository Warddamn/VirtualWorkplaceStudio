**Net: bullet 3 holds.** Two corrections matter for the venture:
1. **Almost no new U.S. DRAM arrives before 2028.** The only U.S. increment in 2026–27 is Manassas 1-alpha DDR4/LP4, and it is aimed at long-lifecycle auto, defense and industrial buyers, not consumers or module makers.
2. **The new U.S. capacity comes on line in 2028–2030.** ID1 reaches meaningful volume in 2028, ID2 in late 2028 and NY1 in 2030. SK hynix's Indiana HBM packaging lands in 2028–29. This matches the 2028–29 glut window that mission §1 bullet 4 warns about.

### 1.5 Crucial consumer exit: dates and reasons

- **Announced:** 2025-12-03 ([StorageNewsletter reprint, 2025-12-05](https://www.storagenewsletter.com/2025/12/05/micron-announces-exit-from-crucial-consumer-business/); [Gigazine, 2025-12-04](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251204-micron-announces-exit-crucial-consumer-business)) [REPORTED].
- **Wind-down:** consumer shipments to retailers, e-tailers and distributors continued **through the end of FQ2 FY2026 (late Feb 2026)**. Warranty and support continue. Affected staff are being redeployed into open positions [REPORTED].
- **What did not exit:** Micron-branded enterprise products continue to be sold to commercial-channel customers worldwide [REPORTED]. The FY2026 10-K confirms that consumer products were sold under Crucial until the December 2025 exit ([10-K summary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm)) [REPORTED-P].
- **Stated reason:** AI-driven data-center growth, and to "improve supply and support for our larger, strategic customers in faster-growing segments" [REPORTED]. This ends a 29-year brand ([CRN Asia, 2025-12](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2025/components-and-peripherals/crucial-s-29-year-run-ends-as-micron-pivots-entirely-to-ente)).
- **Implication:** the U.S. consumer/DIY upgrade market lost its only U.S.-HQ first-party DRAM brand. Retail modules now come from module houses buying Samsung, SK hynix and (increasingly) CXMT die, plus whatever Micron sells through distribution.

### 1.6 Investment tax credit (§48D): now 35%, but the window closes 2026-12-31

- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) raised the §48D advanced manufacturing investment credit from **25% to 35%** for qualified property **placed in service after 2025-12-31** ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/advanced-manufacturing-credits-section-48d); [SIA, 2025-07](https://www.semiconductors.org/?p=29572); [Bloomberg, 2025-07-01](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-01/chipmakers-win-bigger-tax-credit-under-bill-passed-by-senate)) [REPORTED].
- **Construction must begin by 2026-12-31.** OBBBA did not extend the sunset ([Instead, 2026-03-31](https://instead.com/resources/blog/section-48d-credit-rises-to-35-and-why-you-must-act-before-2027)). The BASIC Act (H.R. 3204), which would extend it to 2030, is not shown as enacted ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr3204/BILLS-119hr3204ih.htm)) [REPORTED].
- **For Micron:** ID1, ID2, NY1 and Manassas all began construction before the deadline, so their qualifying tools and buildings placed in service from 2026 earn 35%. On $50–150B of qualifying property that is **$17.5–52.5B of credit, roughly 3–8x the ~$6.44B in grants** [ESTIMATE: 0.35 × assumed qualifying base; see assumptions]. NY fabs 2–4 (construction 2030s) **do not qualify unless Congress extends 48D**.
- **For this venture:** a new U.S. memory fab or packaging line that has not broken ground by **2026-12-31, eight weeks from today**, gets no §48D credit under current law. Any T2/T3 manufacturing concept must assume **0% ITC** unless an extension passes. **This removes a premise that most "build in America" memory pitches rely on.**

## 2. Does Micron sell bare DRAM die, wafers or KGD to third parties?

**Short answer: not openly to small buyers in 2026. There are three narrow paths.** [ESTIMATE / judgment, from the evidence below]

1. **SpecTek (a Micron division).** Micron's own SpecTek support page says SpecTek sells "NAND flash, DRAM and low-power DRAM, in various form factors such as BGA packages, **wafers** and more", positioned as cost-effective, consumer-grade memory ([Micron/SpecTek page](https://micron.net/sales-support/customer-support/spectek-support)) [REPORTED-P]. SpecTek parts contain current Micron die; for example, a SpecTek DDR5 part uses Micron 1-alpha die ([TechInsights](https://www.techinsights.com/blog/spectek-pt040-56b-ddr5-dram-micron-1a-memory-floorplan-analysis)). Historically SpecTek began as Micron's component-recovery and down-bin channel [REPORTED, forum-sourced]. **This is the most plausible door to Micron silicon in wafer form for a small U.S. module or packaging startup**, but terms, minimums and 2026 allocation are unknown. **Kill-test: one email to SpecTek sales asking for wafer or KGD availability, MOQ and lead time.**
2. **Historic KGD programs.** Micron supplied DRAM/SRAM KGD under government KGD criteria. An archived government document lists "Vendor: Micron Technology, Part Function: SRAM & DRAM" ([gov archive PDF](https://corpora.tika.apache.org/base/docs/govdocs1/425/425118.pdf)), and Micron co-developed high-volume KGD handling with Cybex. Both are undated or old [REPORTED]. **No current public Micron KGD catalog surfaced in 2026 searches** [UNVERIFIED that none exists].
3. **Direct strategic agreements.** Micron sells mainly through its own direct sales force, plus independent reps and distributors ([Micron 10-K, historic description](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000110465904030617/a04-11602_110k.htm)). MCP/SiP customers (phones, auto) get die inside Micron-built multi-chip packages, not as bare die. The FY2026 10-K's "supply allocation decisions" language [REPORTED-P] and TrendForce's note that DRAM is being "prioritized for strategic OEMs ... while limiting module makers" ([TrendForce abstract, 2026-01-14](https://www.trendforce.com/research/download/RP260114IU)) mean a startup ranks last.

**Authorized distribution of packaged parts** exists through franchised distributors (Avnet, Mouser, Newark and others), but stock is thin. In May 2026, Newark, Avnet and Mouser all showed **zero authorized stock** of a mainstream Micron DDR4 part (MT40A512M16LY-062E), and independent-market prices were well above authorized levels ([Findchips, 2026-05](https://blog.findchips.com/memory-shortage-watch-ddr3l-ddr4-may-2026/)) [REPORTED]. Micross, which bills itself as the largest authorized bare-die supplier, lists DRAM but does **not** name Micron as a franchise in the material found ([Micross flyer](https://micross.com/uploads/05827c98-1b61-4d4d-bb3c-04dc1b207e5a/Die_and_Wafer_Flyer_Rev_1_WEB.pdf)) [REPORTED].

**Foundry-like or custom DRAM offering:** Micron is **not a foundry**. Its only "custom" product is **custom HBM4E** from 2027: TSMC fabricates the base logic die for both standard and custom HBM4E, and custom logic-die designs are offered to customers who pay a premium. HBM4 (ramping in 2026) keeps Micron's in-house base die ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/micron-hands-tsmc-the-keys-to-hbm4e); [TrendForce, 2026-08-12](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/08/12/news-micron-flags-hbm4e-as-start-of-custom-hbm-era-hbm-market-could-tilt-toward-dual-or-single-suppliers/)) [REPORTED]. This targets hyperscaler and GPU vendors at billions of dollars of volume, not startups.

## 4. U.S. trade policy specific to memory (as of 2026-11-01)

### 4.1 Section 232 semiconductors: Phase 1 is narrow, and memory is effectively not tariffed

- **Proclamation 11002** (2026-01-14, effective **2026-01-15**) imposes a **25%** duty (HTS 9903.79.01) on a narrow class of advanced computing chips. The White House named NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X ([CRS LSB11409](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB11409.html); [Federal Register 2026-01052](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/xml/2026/01/20/2026-01052.xml); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/president-trump-orders-narrowly-targeted-25-section-232-tariff-certain-advanced)) [REPORTED-P].
- **Scope:** covered goods must be classified in **8471.50, 8471.80 or 8473.30** *and* contain a logic IC meeting a **TPP + DRAM-bandwidth window**: TPP 14,000–17,500 with DRAM bandwidth 4,500–5,000 GB/s, or TPP 20,800–21,100 with 5,800–6,200 GB/s. Codes **9903.79.02–.09** give 0% for non-qualifying articles, **U.S. data-center use**, and other end uses ([FedEx Trade Networks guide](https://www.fedex.com/content/dam/fedex/us-united-states/International/Section_232_Import_Duties_on_Semiconductors_and_their_Derivative_Products.pdf)) [REPORTED]. CRS notes the tariff works more like an export-licensing fee than an import adjustment.
- **Memory:** standalone DRAM ICs (8542.32) and ordinary memory modules (8473.30) **do not meet the logic-IC thresholds, so they pay 0% under 232**. Korea's trade minister said Korean memory chips "are currently excluded" ([Korea Times, 2026-09-15](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/opinion/20260915/what-us-tariff-push-means-for-koreas-semiconductor-industry)) [REPORTED]. HBM is taxed only *indirectly*: it is counted in the accelerator's "DRAM bandwidth" test. The "+25%" footnote on 8542.32/8473.30 in HTS Rev. 11 (2026-07-01) is the Chapter-99 cross-reference, not a blanket duty ([HTSHub](https://www.htshub.com/us-hs/search/memory)) [ESTIMATE: interpretation].
- **Phase 2 (broader tariffs plus a "tariff offset program" for U.S. investors) has not been implemented.** Commerce owed a data-center chip market update by 2026-07-01. As of the latest coverage I found (≈May–Sep 2026), Phase 2 "remains under consideration". Commentators speculate it could cover DRAM and NAND ([TariffLens blog](https://www.tarifflens.ai/blog/section-232-semiconductor-tariff-july-2026-phase-2-guide)) [UNVERIFIED speculation]. **Status after Sep 2026 is unknown; re-check the Federal Register.**
- **The 100% memory tariff threat:** at Micron's NY groundbreaking (2026-01-16), Lutnick said memory makers must "build in America" or "the tariff is likely to be 100 percent" ([TrendForce, 2026-01-19](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/01/19/news-lutnick-reportedly-signals-100-memory-tariff-on-non-u-s-output-pressuring-samsung-and-sk-hynix/); [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/01/19/2003850817)) [REPORTED]. **Not implemented** as of the latest reporting. Nine U.S. trade associations wrote on 2026-06-03 warning about memory supply ([KuCoin aggregator](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/us-commerce-secretary-pressures-samsung-and-sk-hynix-to-build-memory-chip-production-in-the-us)) [REPORTED, weak].
- **Background:** the Section 232 semiconductor investigation opened in April 2025, and in August 2025 the President floated a ~100% chip tariff with exemptions for companies building in the U.S. [UNVERIFIED: prior knowledge].

### 4.2 Country deals: Taiwan and Korea

- **Taiwan (2026-01-15):** overall U.S. duties on Taiwanese goods capped at **15%** (down from 20%). Taiwanese firms building U.S. chip capacity may import **2.5x their planned U.S. capacity duty-free under 232 during construction** and **1.5x after completion**, with preferential rates above the quota. Taiwan pledged ≥$250B of U.S. investment. The deal is subject to Legislative Yuan review, and no effective date was given at signing ([Manufacturing Dive, 2026-01-15](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/us-taiwan-ink-tariff-deal/809829/); [Baker McKenzie, 2026-01-20](https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/01/20/us-and-taiwan-finalize-trade-agreement-with-focus-on-boosting-semiconductor-collaboration/)) [REPORTED]. **This is the template for any "tariff offset" regime: U.S. capacity buys duty-free import quota.**
- **Korea:** the Nov 2025 joint fact sheet gives Korea chip tariff terms "no less favorable" than comparable deals (i.e., Taiwan). Seoul invoked that clause in Jan 2026 ([Korea Herald, 2026-01](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10657692)). As of Sep 2026 Korea has the *promise* but not Taiwan-style specific exemptions ([Korea Times, 2026-09-15](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/opinion/20260915/what-us-tariff-push-means-for-koreas-semiconductor-industry)) [REPORTED]. SK hynix's Indiana plant (packaging only) may not count as "new chip capacity" for quota purposes [ESTIMATE].

### 4.3 "Reciprocal" tariffs and their replacements, and how memory fares

| Period | Regime | Memory treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 2025 – Feb 2026 | IEEPA "reciprocal" tariffs (Korea 15%, Taiwan 20%, later capped at 15%) | Semiconductors (8542) were in Annex II. Computers, memory modules (8473.30) and SSDs were exempted by CBP guidance in April 2025 [UNVERIFIED: prior knowledge] |
| **2026-02-20** | **SCOTUS 6–3 (*Learning Resources v. Trump*): IEEPA does not authorize tariffs.** IEEPA tariffs terminated ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/2/supreme-court-decides-learning-resources-inc-v-trump-and-trump-v-vos-selections-inc)) | — |
| 2026-02-24 → **2026-07-24** | Section 122 global surcharge of 10% (statutory maximum 15%, 150 days). Expired 2026-07-24 ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/02/ieepa-tariffs-terminated-replacement-section-122-tariffs-take-effect); [UHY, 2026-07](https://uhy-us.com/insights/news/2026/july/tariff-reset-section-122-expires-as-section-301-duties-expand)) | Memory-specific carve-outs not confirmed [UNVERIFIED] |
| **2026-07-24 → now** | **Section 301 "forced-labor" tariffs** on 60 economies (99.4% of imports), mostly **10–12.5%**. Goods under 232 and USMCA-compliant goods excluded. Litigation expected ([UHY](https://uhy-us.com/insights/news/2026/july/tariff-reset-section-122-expires-as-section-301-duties-expand); [BorderBuddy](https://borderbuddy.com/trade-tariff-news/section-122-is-out-section-301-is-in)) | **Whether DRAM and modules from Korea and Taiwan pay 10–12.5% is unconfirmed** [UNVERIFIED]. **Effect on DRAM and module imports today [ESTIMATE, from the above]:** MFN duty on DRAM ICs (8542.32) and modules (8473.30) is **Free**. Phase-1 232 does not reach memory. The only *confirmed* memory-specific duty is the 50% on China-origin chips. So **as of Nov 2026, tariffs give U.S.-made memory no structural price advantage.** Any "Made in America" premium has to come from procurement rules (NDAA §5949 from Dec 2027; DoD/trusted supply), lifecycle guarantees, or a future Phase-2 232. All of these are policy bets, not current law.

### 4.4 Other memory-relevant U.S. rules

- **NDAA FY2023 §5949:** a federal procurement ban on products containing CXMT, YMTC or SMIC chips, effective **Dec 2027** [REPORTED]. This creates a compliance need, *provenance-verified non-Chinese memory*, across federal contractors' supply chains. **It is the most concrete U.S.-policy demand signal for a small memory venture** (traceability, test and certification) [ESTIMATE: judgment].
- **Export controls:** DRAM tool controls (≤18nm half-pitch) since Oct 2022. The BIS 50% affiliates rule (late 2025) expands Entity-List reach ([Simpson Thacher via JDSupra, 2025-10](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2025/10/17/simpson-thacher-attorneys-author-i-law360-i-article-on-bis-50-rule-for-entity-list-and-other-export-restrictive-lists)) [REPORTED, title only].

## 6. What this means for American Memory (decision-relevant)

1. **Timing.** U.S. DRAM supply barely moves until 2028. Then ID1, ID2, NY1 (2030), SK hynix Indiana and overseas fabs all ramp into the 2028–30 window, where mission §1 bullet 4 expects a glut. Micron has pre-sold ~35% of revenue through 2030, so the spot and channel market (module makers, DIY) absorbs more of any downturn. **Concepts that depend on the current shortage must reach payback before 2028.**
2. **Legacy-DRAM niche is real but Micron is filling part of it.** Manassas 1-alpha DDR4/LP4 is aimed at auto, defense, aerospace and industrial buyers. TrendForce still expects legacy shortages to persist, and buffers sit at 6–8 weeks. A trailing-node DRAM concept competes with Micron Manassas for the same "U.S.-made, long-lifecycle" buyer, and Micron has a CHIPS-funded 1-alpha head start.
3. **No Micron die for startups.** Bare die and wafers from Micron are effectively unavailable to small buyers (allocation, no public KGD program). SpecTek wafers are the one door worth knocking on. Taiwanese specialty-DRAM KGD (Winbond, ESMT, Etron) is the realistic supply for U.S. module or SiP assembly.
4. **Tariff moat: not yet.** Memory is untaxed under 232 Phase 1. The 100% threat is unimplemented. The IEEPA regime is gone, and the replacement 301 treatment of memory is unclear. Only China-origin chips pay 50%. Price any "Made in USA" premium at zero until Phase 2 is published.
5. **§48D closes for newcomers on 2026-12-31.** Capital-heavy concepts get no 35% ITC unless Congress extends it (BASIC Act pending). Model T2/T3 concepts at 0% ITC.
6. **Procurement pull is the strongest U.S.-policy lever for a small firm.** The §5949 ban on CXMT/YMTC-containing products in federal buys from Dec 2027, combined with Apple-style pressure to use CXMT commercially, creates demand for **provenance, test and certification of non-Chinese memory**. This is software- and test-heavy and fits the founder better.
7. **Crucial's exit removed the only U.S.-HQ first-party consumer DRAM brand** (completed Feb 2026). U.S. retail and upgrade buyers now depend on module houses that source Samsung, SK hynix and CXMT die. A U.S.-assembled, provenance-verified consumer/prosumer module line is a coherent wedge, but die access (point 3) is its choke point.
