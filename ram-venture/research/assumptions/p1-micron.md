# Assumptions — p1-micron (Micron and U.S. memory-maker footprint, U.S. memory trade policy)

Session constraints that affect every fact below: WebFetch and direct HTTPS to sec.gov, investors.micron.com, micron.gcs-web.com, trendforce.com and blocksandfiles.com were refused by the session's network policy. The shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through the task. So most facts come from search-result snippets, including snippets of primary documents (SEC filings, NIST, Federal Register), and none are tagged [VERIFIED].

- [p1-micron] Snippets of Micron's FY2026 10-K (period ended 2026-09-03) quoted in search results are accurate to the filing. — basis: several independent searches returned the same quoted sentences (ID1 mid-CY2027, ID2 late CY2028, NY initial wafer output CY2030, CHIPS up to $6.1B unchanged).
- [p1-micron] When the 10-K conflicts with press coverage (e.g., a NY "operations Q1 2029" figure), the 10-K wins. — basis: it is the most recent primary disclosure (filed ~Oct 2026).
- [p1-micron] "First wafer output" ≠ meaningful supply. Volume qualified output from ID1 is assumed for 2028. — basis: a Micron marketing VP quoted by OC3D (2026); fab ramps typically take 12–18 months from first wafer to high-volume production.
- [p1-micron] Total Micron CHIPS direct funding = $6.165B (ID/NY, final 2024-12-10) + $275M (Manassas, final June 2025) ≈ $6.44B. — basis: NIST/Commerce award announcements and Micron's June 2025 release, both via snippet. I found no reduction or equity conversion (unlike Intel's 2025 deal). Absence of evidence is not proof.
- [p1-micron] Micron's global DRAM bit share is 22–26% in 2026. — basis: historical TrendForce/Counterpoint ranges (Micron about 20–25%). Not re-verified this session because the search budget ran out.
- [p1-micron] Manassas = 3–8% of Micron's DRAM bit output in 2026. — basis: TrendForce says DDR4 is ~7% of Micron output in 2026 after the line transfer to Fab 6 (Manassas). Manassas also runs LP4. Older nodes yield fewer bits per wafer. This is a bounding assumption, not a measured value.
- [p1-micron] The U.S. supplies 15–35% of Micron's DRAM bits in 2030 (ID1 ramped, ID2 ramping, NY1 at initial output, Manassas). — basis: company timelines. Micron's 40% U.S. goal is "long-term" and tied to the >$250B plan through 2035, so 2030 sits below 40%.
- [p1-micron] The Boise R&D fab's output is negligible for national share. — basis: it is a development fab. Judgment call.
- [p1-micron] 48D (35%) illustration: I assume $50–150B of 48D-qualifying property placed in service across ID1/ID2/NY1 over the decade. — basis: the $200–250B plan includes about $50B of R&D, which does not qualify, plus buildings and land. This is an order-of-magnitude illustration only.
- [p1-micron] The 48D begin-construction deadline is 2026-12-31 and was not extended as of the latest snippets (Mar 2026). — basis: Baker Tilly, Instead and BASIC Act status. If Congress extends it after my sources, the conclusions on NY fabs 2–4 and on new entrants change.
- [p1-micron] Memory ICs (HTS 8542.32) and memory modules (8473.30) are not subject to the Phase-1 Section 232 duty in practice. — basis: Phase-1 scope requires TPP plus DRAM-bandwidth thresholds tied to a logic IC (FedEx/White & Case), and Korea's trade minister said memory is excluded. The "+25%" footnote in HTS Rev. 11 is a Chapter-99 cross-reference, not a blanket duty.
- [p1-micron] Under the 2025 IEEPA "reciprocal" regime, semiconductors (8542) and computer parts including memory modules (8473.30) and SSDs were exempt (Annex II, then CBP guidance of April 2025). — basis: prior knowledge, not re-verified this session.
- [p1-micron] The Section 301 forced-labor tariffs in force since 2026-07-24 (10–12.5%) may or may not exempt memory. — basis: snippets say only that goods under Section 232 are excluded. Memory-specific treatment was not found, so it is flagged [UNVERIFIED].
- [p1-micron] China-origin DRAM (8542.32) carries a 50% Section 301 duty. — basis: the 2024 four-year-review increase on Chinese semiconductors (prior knowledge) plus a tariff aggregator snippet showing 9903.91.05 +50%.
- [p1-micron] SK hynix Indiana CHIPS award = up to $458M in grants plus up to $500M in loans (finalized Dec 2024). — basis: prior knowledge. Not re-verified this session because the search budget ran out. Tagged [UNVERIFIED].
- [p1-micron] Kioxia, SanDisk, Western Digital, Solidigm, Nanya and Winbond have no U.S. memory wafer fabs. They have only U.S. HQs or sales offices. — basis: prior knowledge plus the absence of any U.S. fab in 2026 search coverage. Lutnick-era reporting says Micron is the only company producing or planning DRAM in the U.S.
- [p1-micron] Micron HBM share in Q2 2026 is about 17%. — basis: subtraction from Counterpoint figures (SK hynix 50%, Samsung 33%) quoted in a SammyFans snippet. If a fourth supplier or rounding matters, the value is ±3 pts.
- [p1-micron] Micron FY2025 revenue = $37.38B, used to check the reported +256% FY2026 growth (133.2/37.38 = 3.56x). — basis: prior knowledge. The check is internally consistent.
- [p1-micron] Micron does not run an open, catalog-based bare-DRAM-die (KGD) program for small third parties in 2026. The realistic Micron-silicon die path is SpecTek (wafers, consumer grade) or a direct strategic agreement. — basis: no current KGD catalog page surfaced, the FY2026 10-K describes supply-allocation decisions, and SpecTek's support page lists wafers.
