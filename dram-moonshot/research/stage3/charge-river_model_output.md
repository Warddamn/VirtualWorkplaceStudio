## 4. Charge-transfer efficiency -> BER -> minimum CTE

Model: per-transfer deferral eps = k_ws x (1 - CTE); after n_t transfers a '1' keeps h0 = (1-eps)^n_t and the rest trails into later packets. The noise is read noise plus fat-zero noise, plus binomial partition noise N_s h0 (1-h0) and Poisson noise on the deferred charge. Partition noise is physics that no correction removes. Without DFE the threshold is fixed: the worst '1' follows zeros and the worst '0' follows ones, so the eye is (2 h0 - 1) N_s. With DFE the threshold follows the decided history, and the eye is h0 N_s - 2 kappa x tail (LSST-style deferred-charge correction, [Snyder 2021](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/Journal-of-Astronomical-Telescopes-Instruments-and-Systems/volume-7/issue-04/048002/Characterization-and-correction-of-serial-deferred-charge-in-LSST-camera/10.1117/1.JATIS.7.4.048002.full)).

## 5. Trap kinetics: carrying imec's 6.25 MHz point to a 50 MHz stage clock

Transport is not the limit. Drift across a 25 nm gate at 10 cm^2/Vs and 1 V takes ~6.2e-13 s, and diffusion takes ~2.4e-11 s, both far below the 6.7 ns dwell at 50 MHz. Loss comes from traps that capture electrons while the packet sits and emit them after it has moved two gates on. With SRH emission tau_e = exp(E/kT) / nu0, nu0 = sigma v_th N_c = 1.1e+11 /s (v_th 2.1e+07 cm/s), the gates that lose charge are those with tau_e near the dwell time, i.e. trap depth E_w = kT ln(nu0 t_dwell).

## 7. Latency and schedulability

Effective latency = t_hit + (1 - s) x T_lap / 2, where s is the share of bytes whose address is known at least one lap ahead. Required s for a 40 ns average with t_hit = 15 ns:

| 25.00 MHz | 1.28 us | 96.1% | 99.5% -> 18 ns | 93.4% -> 58 ns |
| 50.00 MHz | 0.64 us | 92.2% | 99.5% -> 17 ns | 96.7% -> 26 ns |

| 200.00 MHz | 0.16 us | 68.8% | 99.5% -> 15 ns | 98.0% -> 17 ns |

Workload per decode step (dense 70B FP8, batch 32, 8192 context): weights 70 GB + KV 42.9 GB, all in a fixed or append-then-scan order. **Finding:** an access only has to be known ONE LAP ahead (0.64 us at 50 MHz), and a decode layer takes tens of microseconds, so most routed-expert and top-k KV reads are schedulable at 50 MHz. With a 0.5% random floor (dense decode), even imec's 4-6 MHz clock meets 40 ns. With MoE + sparse attention (2% random floor, microsecond lead times), 4-6 MHz gives 0.7-1.5 us averages while 50 MHz gives ~26 ns. That is the real case for the 50 MHz clock. ## 12. GO / NO-GO

**GO/NO-GO:** GO if planar test CCDs (sheet charge ~4.5e+12 cm^-2, i.e. <= 447 e- per 40 nm-plate packet; worst-case 1-after-0s pattern) show a median per-transfer CTE >= **0.9970 with DFE** (deferral <= 3.0e-03 per transfer) at a >= 50 MHz STAGE clock (>= 150 M transfers/s) at BOTH 25 C and 85 C, and a 98-transfer loop with FPGA regeneration logs raw BER <= 5e-10. The chief's 0.999 spec is the STRONG-GO line: it buys 64-stage segments or 3x the worst-string spread. NO-GO if every channel variant stays below CTE 0.9934 at >= 50 M transfers/s, where density drops under 1.5x 1-beta and cost rises to ~$1.29/GB, close to DDR5 parity. System half: GO if trace replay shows >= 92% of bytes known >= one lap (0.64 us) ahead.

Headline: 1.62 Gb/mm^2 user (3.6x 1-beta), ~$0.71/GB (2.1x below DDR5), 313 e- packets (167 mV at the FD, 8 e- noise), ~0.07 pJ/bit core read, scrub power ~191x below DDR5 refresh, and a minimum CTE of 0.9970 (DFE) / 0.9979 (no DFE) at 98 transfers.
