# Phase 3 scoring rubric — calibration anchors

Score each criterion **1–5, where 5 is always best for the venture**. Use the anchors below so different scorers and batches land on the same scale. Base every score on the card, the verify notes, `01_LANDSCAPE.md`, and the device cost model where it applies. Give a one-line justification with a specific fact for every score. **Score for THIS founder and THIS venture, not for Samsung.**

| Key | Criterion (default weight) | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tech` | Technical feasibility (20) | Physics shaky, or TRL ≤ 3; no credible path inside 5 years | Demonstrated in lab/prototype (TRL 4–6); real engineering risk remains | Proven, shipping technology (TRL 8–9), or software built with known techniques; integration risk only |
| `capital` | Capital required, inverse (15) | T3/T4 (> $50M before meaningful revenue) | High T1 ($0.5–2M) — 4 = low T1 (< $500k); 2 = T2 ($2–50M) | T0: first revenue reachable on < $50k |
| `ttr` | Time to first revenue (15) | > 36 months | 13–18 months (4 = 7–12; 2 = 19–36) | ≤ 6 months |
| `usmfg` | U.S. manufacturability (10) | Only possible offshore | Software/services (no fab dependency, no U.S. manufacturing added), or U.S. assembly of imported die (4 = mostly U.S. value-add, e.g. U.S. MPW fab + U.S. assembly; 2 = needs U.S. capacity that doesn't take outside customers or doesn't exist yet) | Physical product made in the U.S. with partners that take outside customers today |
| `margin` | Gross margin (10) | < 15% | 30–50% (4 = 50–70%; 2 = 15–30%) | ≥ 70% |
| `moat` | Moat (10) | None; a commodity anyone can copy | Some: know-how, relationships, certifications, early-customer lock-in | Strong: owned/licensed exclusive IP, qualification lock-in, regulatory status, network effects |
| `competition` | Competitive density, inverse (5) | Crowded with well-funded incumbents doing exactly this | A few players, room for a niche | No credible player in this exact niche |
| `regulatory` | Regulatory/export risk, inverse (5) | High: legal gray zone (e.g. counterfeit exposure) or export-controlled in the core market | Manageable: ITAR registration, counterfeit-avoidance compliance, AS6081, etc. | Negligible |
| `cycle` | Cycle-proofing (5) | Dies in a 2028–29 glut (DDR5 −60%) | Revenue shrinks materially but the business survives | Indifferent to the glut or benefits from it (counter-cyclical or structural demand) |
| `founder` | Founder-fit (5) | Needs deep semiconductor process expertise and a full-time team from day one | Needs 1–2 key domain hires or a cofounder; founder adds real value | Squarely in the founder's skills (ships software fast); can start part-time alongside the day job |

## Calibration reminders
- **Score possibility, profitability and founder-doability separately.** `tech` is "could anyone build it". `founder` is "can this founder". `margin`/`moat` are "is it a business".
- **Don't let enthusiasm inflate `moat`.** "First mover" alone is a 2, and open-source software you could copy over a weekend is a 1.
- **`cycle` applies to the specific revenue line.** A tool that sells *because* of the shortage (price tracker, recert brokerage) usually scores 1–2. Selling cost-savings that still matter at lower prices scores 3–4.
- **`ttr` counts customer revenue, not grants** (ASSUMPTIONS A-04).
- **Expect the spread to use the whole scale.** If most of your scores are 3–4, you are not discriminating.
