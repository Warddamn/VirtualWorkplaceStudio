## Cost-model conventions
- [A3] Usable die area = 85% of gross wafer area (300mm: 60,083 mm²; 200mm: 26,704 mm²), covering edge exclusion and scribe — basis: common rule of thumb [ESTIMATE].
- [A3] die_density_Gb_per_mm2 = (1/cell_area_um2) × 1e6 × array_efficiency × layers × bits_per_cell / 1e9 (same formula as A1) — basis: consistency with A1_device_params.
- [A3] Test+package cost per die: $1.5 for a DRAM-like 100 mm² standalone die; $3 for a 200 mm² SRAM die; $1.5 for a small 16Mb SRAM in TSOP/BGA. $/GB = per-die cost / GB per die — basis: [ESTIMATE: OSAT lore; consistent with A2's $1.0–2.5/die].
- [A3] Array efficiency: 0.70 for large standalone SRAM, 0.65 for gain-cell macros, 0.50 for capacitor-less standalone DRAM, 0.45 for 1T1C eDRAM — basis: [ESTIMATE: typical sense-amp/decoder overheads; DRAM ~0.55 in A1].

## Device-parameter assumptions
- [A3] Published 6T SRAM bitcells: 65nm 0.525 µm²; 40nm 0.242 µm²; 28nm 0.127 µm²; 16nm 0.074 µm²; N5 0.021 µm²; N2 macro density 38.1 Mb/mm² (TSMC IEDM 2024) — basis: [UNVERIFIED-training: foundry and IEDM disclosures]. Only the SkyWater 130nm cell (1.896 µm²) was verified from the PDK LEF.
- [A3] 130nm 200mm SRAM for long-lifecycle parts uses a 2.0 µm² cell — basis: close to the verified sky130 1.896 µm² cell.
- [A3] Floating-body 1T-DRAM cell is 8F² at 45nm PD-SOI (0.0162 µm²) — basis: [ESTIMATE; ISi and Toshiba FBC claimed 4–8F² in planar SOI]. In ultra-thin FD-SOI (<10 nm Si) the body holds too little charge for reliable sensing — basis: [UNVERIFIED-training; device-physics argument].
- [A3] Thyristor RAM (TCCT) cell is 10F² at 32nm SOI. Kilopass VLT claimed 4F² — basis: [UNVERIFIED-training: T-RAM and Kilopass marketing claims]. Holding current is 1–100 pA/cell in the ON state, so a 1 Gb array idles at 1–110 mA — basis: [ESTIMATE, computed].
- [A3] Si 2T/3T gain cell at 28nm is ≈0.065 µm² (≈50% of HD 6T). Retention is ≈10–100 µs at 85°C, rising to ms at 25°C — basis: [UNVERIFIED-training: RAAAM "~2x denser than SRAM" claim; Teman/Giterman/Burg gain-cell literature].
- [A3] IBM 14nm SOI eDRAM cell is 0.0174 µm² (IEDM 2014). Intel 22nm eDRAM cell is 0.029 µm², and the 128MB Crystalwell die is ≈77 mm² — basis: [UNVERIFIED-training].
- [A3] Nantero NRAM is 1T1R at 28nm, transistor-limited at ≈30F² — basis: [ESTIMATE]. Nantero claims of 5 ns switching, >1e11 cycles and >10-year retention at high temperature are [UNVERIFIED-training].
- [A3] Cryogenic leakage reduction raises Si gain-cell retention by ≥100–1000× at 77 K and 4 K — basis: [UNVERIFIED-training: cryo-CMOS eDRAM literature 2020–2024].
- [A3] Thinfilm printed memory label holds 20 bits and costs ≈ $0.10, i.e. ≈ $4e7/GB — basis: [ESTIMATE; Thinfilm 20-bit Memory product from training data].

## Company and market facts used (all [UNVERIFIED-training] unless noted)
- [A3] Innovative Silicon (Z-RAM) licensed AMD (2006) and Hynix (2007), closed in 2010, and its patents went to Micron.
- [A3] T-RAM Semiconductor licensed AMD (2006) and partnered with GlobalFoundries (2009, 32/22nm SOI). It was defunct by about 2010–11.
- [A3] Kilopass announced the VLT thyristor DRAM in 2016. Synopsys acquired Kilopass in early 2018, and VLT was never commercialized.
- [A3] IBM POWER7/8/9 and z-series used deep-trench eDRAM on 45/32/22/14nm SOI. POWER10 and z16 (Samsung 7nm) dropped eDRAM for SRAM. GF sold East Fishkill (ex-IBM 300mm) to onsemi, completed end-2022.
- [A3] Groq's first-gen LPU is ≈230 MB SRAM on GlobalFoundries 14nm (Malta, NY), ≈725 mm². In December 2025 Groq and NVIDIA announced a non-exclusive technology license plus executive hires, reported at ≈ $20B; GroqCloud continues.
- [A3] Cerebras WSE-3: 44 GB on-chip SRAM, TSMC 5nm, 46,225 mm² (Mar 2024). Cerebras withdrew its IPO filing in Oct 2025 after a $1.1B raise. Large OpenAI compute deal in Jan 2026.
