# Assumptions — p4-memory-efficiency (DD4, as of 2026-11-01)

Each numeric value also sits in the INPUTS block of `models/memory-efficiency.py`, with its source or basis.

- [p4-memory-efficiency] Month 1 of the model is 2026-11 and month 36 is 2029-10. The DRAM turn starts in 2028-01, which is month 15, so it lands *before* month 18. — basis: 01_LANDSCAPE F2 (downturn most likely 2028); task spec (GLUT from 2028-Q1).
- [p4-memory-efficiency] BASE scenario: the 64GB DDR5 RDIMM holds at $25.78 per user-GB through 2027, then falls 30% over 2028 and stays flat. — basis: midpoint between BofA's −10% 2028 ASP call and the A-05 −60% glut.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] GLUT scenario: the price ramps over 12 months from 2028-01 down to $10.31 per user-GB (A-05). SEVERE scenario: the same ramp, down to $2.04/GB component, which is $2.58 per user-GB at the RDIMM/component ratio of 1.266 from `ddr5_reference()`. — basis: 2018-19 analog, −62.5% in about 4 quarters.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] Used DDR4 RDIMM is worth $4.45/GB (midpoint of $3.1–5.8). It falls 30% in BASE, 50% in GLUT (floored at the $1.30 trough) and to $1.30 in SEVERE. — basis: amcompute via p3-audit-11; A-05 (DDR4 falls less in %).
- [p4-memory-efficiency] C-74 frees **10% of installed RAM** on a third-party fleet. That sits between Google's ~5% of fleet RAM from zswap and Meta TMO's 20–32%. TMO's figure relies on in-house application tuning plus SSD offload, which a vendor working on a stranger's fleet will not get on day one. — basis: Google ASPLOS'19/LPC talk; TMO ASPLOS'22; DAMON_RECLAIM lab results of 32–40% (not production).
- [p4-memory-efficiency] The reference node has 512 GB of RAM, of which 60% of node-GB is DDR5 (the targeting rule) and 40% is DDR4 valued at used-DIMM prices. A customer has 80 nodes on average. — basis: the C-74 card's reference fleet and the p3-audit DDR4 correction.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] The C-74 price is a **30% share** of the deferred-DRAM value spread over a 36-month term, capped at a $10/node/month list price. Repricing lags the DRAM price by 6 months because contracts are annual. — basis: ProsperOps (~30–35%) and Zesty (25%) savings-share rates; the per-node comparables are Kubecost (~$4.5/node), StormForge ($3/vCPU), CAST AI ($5/vCPU) and Datadog ($15/host).
- [p4-memory-efficiency] The C-74 funnel converts reply 8% → scan 35% → pilot 40% → close 60%, about 0.67% of outbound touches. The founder sends 40 touches/month part-time or 120 full-time. An open-source scanner adds inbound leads that ramp to 4 conversations/month between months 9 and 15. A "pain" multiplier of 0.4 + 0.6 × (RDIMM price ÷ peak) scales conversion. — basis: ESTIMATE; no on-prem benchmark was found.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] C-74 churn is 1.5%/month at base, plus 2.0% × (1 − price ÷ peak) once DRAM gets cheap. — basis: card glut_reason ("buyers just buy RAM").
- [p4-memory-efficiency] C-74 COGS: $0.25/node/month hosting; 10% of subscription revenue for support; a kernel-savvy on-call retainer of $750/month (1–5 customers) or $2,000/month (6+ customers); $2,100 of contractor time per pilot against a $2,000 pilot fee. Free scans ($200 each) are booked as sales and marketing. — basis: ESTIMATE; the card says one incident ends the company, so on-call is not optional.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] The C-74 build takes 100 h of a kernel contractor at $175/h ($17.5k) over months 2–5. The card's ~300 h was scoped down to an MVP that fits inside $40k. — basis: the p3-audit-11 capital-tier correction.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] C-73: a $3,000 audit, with the first one in month 5. The part-time rate is 0.33 audits/month and the full-time rate is 1/month. 10% of audits convert to $250/month monitoring, which churns at 6%/month. — basis: p3-audit-11 (4–8 months to the first audit; the founder's network is in manufacturing, not SaaS; AWS list prices unchanged, so no pain).
- [p4-memory-efficiency] C-75: a $25k engagement whose COGS is $10k of contractor and GPU time. The first engagement can close in month 9. The deal rate is 0.15/month part-time and 0.35 full-time, decaying linearly to 30% of that by month 24. 60% of engagements convert to a $1,500/month retainer with 4% monthly churn. Deal flow falls 20% in GLUT and SEVERE. — basis: Red Hat AI 3.5 CPU KV offload GA (2026-09-09), Tensormesh Platform (2026-10-15) and NVIDIA Dynamo (free) are commoditizing the integration.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] The founder goes full-time only once trailing-3-month (gross profit − non-salary opex) is at least the $10k/month loaded salary. — basis: A-03.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] SOC 2 Type I costs $12k, triggered at the 5th cumulative C-74 customer. — basis: ESTIMATE. On-prem buyers ask for it later than SaaS buyers, but they do ask.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] The model works in expected values, so customers can be fractional. Taxes, payment terms and bad debt are ignored. — basis: simplicity. Read the output as a central case.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] Judgment call: **C-73 is not a lead magnet for C-74.** Cloud SaaS buyers and on-prem operators are different people. C-73 is modeled as a small cash bridge that yields only 0.25 C-74 conversations per audit. The real lead magnet for on-prem buyers is a free, read-only "cold-memory scan" (DAMON/PSI/idle-page statistics, no changes made).
- [p4-memory-efficiency] Judgment call: §5949 may also stop federal *grant/loan* funds from buying CXMT-containing products from 2027-12-23, which would keep "clean" DRAM dearer for federally funded university clusters in a CXMT-led glut. This is **[UNVERIFIED]**; it is noted as a lead and not modeled.
- [p4-memory-efficiency] Judgment call: reaching hosting providers' renters (OVHcloud and Hetzner customers, who now pay RAM pass-through increases) through a C-73-style audit is an untested variant. It is not modeled.
